## Failure decisions: how we avoid stagnation

| Failure or finding | Next action | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| Executed work cannot be attributed from direct links/counters | One bounded causal repair with a new contract and one fresh capability check; consider event-log evidence as a distinct preregistered route | Do not repeat identical runs hoping metadata appears; no CPU attribution claim while unresolved |
| Dependency inventory remains incomplete but executed work reconciles | Report both dimensions; prospective independent audit must establish the narrower claim explicitly | Never label missing references as skipped individually or upgrade historical failures |
| Observation cost consumes potential gain | Evaluate asynchronous/incremental capture with measured freshness, loss and full overhead | Reject a cheaper observer if evidence or freshness is inadequate |
| Compaction changes layout but not query cost | Examine preregistered fragmented-layout and load strata, then report the no-benefit boundary | Do not train a complex controller without a measurable mechanism |
| Timing makes no difference after matched full-drain accounting | Stop timing-model expansion; evaluate an explicitly preregistered resource tradeoff if actual counters support it | Do not choose a new primary metric after seeing favorable results |
| Simple rule matches/beats ML | Keep the simpler rule, report crossover conditions and overhead | Do not tune on reserved test failures |
| New physical test fails | Preserve raw evidence and attempted/unattempted counts; separate system failure from hypothesis result | No fitting from partial failed episodes and no favorable retries |

No new performance campaign is authorized by this document alone without its registered inputs and admission checks. The user's implementation authorization permits preparing the next concrete unit; it does not make missing evidence a pass. Publication feasibility remains conditional on a reproducible effect or a well-supported boundary result, not on having a trained model.
